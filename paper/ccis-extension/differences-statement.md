# Statement of differences between the conference version and the extended version
(for the PRIMORIS submission form; INSTICC requirement 6)

The conference paper (Chauhan and Hegde, DATA 2026, "Chunking Is Not the Bottleneck", DOI 10.5220/0015150400004091) reported a 32-configuration factorial experiment on Natural Questions and MS-MARCO showing that chunking strategy explains only 1–2% of retrieval-quality variance, a 2×2 heading-prefix ablation on Natural Questions, and an end-to-end LLM-judge evaluation. The extended version keeps that experiment only as a cited baseline (regime R1) and asks the follow-up question the conference paper left open: under what corpus conditions does chunking matter? It adds two new experimental regimes run with the same design and a third embedding model: 24 technical documents across eight domains with exact evidence-span labels (576 core configurations plus 144 ablation configurations), and the four LegalBench-RAG legal sub-benchmarks with expert-annotated gold spans (96 core configurations plus 48 with two new legal-aware structure chunkers). It further adds analyses that did not exist in the conference version: strategy-by-corpus and strategy-by-regime interaction tests, partial and within-corpus effect sizes, bootstrap confidence intervals, Holm-corrected significance, paired Cliff's delta contrasts, an analysis relating the chunking effect to corpus features (heading density, document length, evidence length), a floor-effect check for the legal regime, a boundary-versus-prefix ablation on both new regimes, a legal-aware structure parser, a controlled comparison of fuzzy versus exact relevance labels on identical retrieval runs, an end-to-end LLM-judge generation evaluation on the technical regime (1,000 judged answers), and a cost analysis.

The central findings are new: chunking strategy is a significant factor on technical documentation (4.5–8% of pooled variance; median 20% within a document) but not on legal documents (under 1% pooled; median 8% within a sub-benchmark); the strategy-by-corpus interaction exceeds the pooled main effect of strategy on the ranking metrics; and heading density, not document length, is the corpus feature associated with a large chunking effect. The title, abstract, introduction, related work (about 30 new references from 2024–2026), methods, discussion, implications, limitations and conclusions have been rewritten around this question. The only material carried over is the summary of the R1 experiment and its η² values, reproduced in one table column and one figure panel with citation. Less than 1% of the extended text overlaps verbatim with the conference paper; the body grows by about 60% (8 two-column SCITEPRESS pages to 19 LNCS pages).
